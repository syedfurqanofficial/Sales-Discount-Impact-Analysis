Project Explanation: Sales Impact Analysis & Strategy Proposal

This set of files presents a complete business intelligence project conducted for a retail company. The project’s goal was to understand the real impact of discounts on sales, customer loyalty, and overall business performance, and to translate those insights into actionable strategies.

The work is structured into three key documents:

1. 03-SQL Analysis.sql

This is the technical backbone of the project. It begins by setting up a database and creating a detailed sales table containing information on orders, customers, products, discounts, and customer behavior (like session duration and pages viewed).

The SQL script then systematically answers a series of business questions in three phases:

- Phase 1 (Basic Performance): It calculates total revenue, identifies top-selling product categories, and analyzes customer demographics (age, gender) and platform use (mobile vs. desktop). This establishes a baseline understanding of "what" is happening in the business.

- Phase 2 (Discount & Behavior Deep Dive): This is the core analytical section. It measures the precise impact of discounts, revealing a powerful insight: discounts caused a 54.56% increase in sales volume. Crucially, it then analyzes whether these discounted customers are valuable in the long term. The data shows they are—they return to shop again at the same high rate (~86%) as full-price customers and give virtually identical product ratings. Additional queries explore how browsing behavior (time on site, pages viewed) correlates with purchases and satisfaction.

- Phase 3 (Strategic Synthesis): The final queries (framed as questions 10-12) are designed to bridge analysis to strategy. They prompt an investigation into *why* sales fluctuate by month and *what* drives a customer to become loyal. The answers to these, derived from the Phase 2 insights, form the basis for the strategic recommendations.

2. 04-Detailed Analysis of Data.pdf

This report translates the raw SQL findings into clear, business-friendly insights. It presents the key results visually with tables and follows each with a plain-English "Analysis" or "Conclusion" section.

The report’s narrative is compelling and direct. It confidently challenges common business fears about discounts, using data as proof:

- Discounts do not harm loyalty or brand perception. The repeat purchase rate and average rating for discounted customers are statistically the same as for regular customers.

- Mobile is the dominant sales channel, responsible for over 55% of orders.

- The highest customer satisfaction comes from quick, decisive buyers (1-4 minute sessions), not from those who browse the longest.

It concludes by directly answering the Phase 3 questions: sales increase when the proven "engine" of discounts, mobile optimization, and high engagement is activated. Sales decline when this engine is not running. Loyal customers are created by a great first experience—where a discount acts as a risk-free invitation, and product quality ensures their return.

3. 05-Data-Backed Strategy Proposal.pdf

This document is the final deliverable—the actionable plan. It starts by reframing the client’s original worries into four clear strategic questions. It then answers them with a concise, three-part strategy derived directly from the data:

Part 1: The Growth Engine. Recommendation: Use discounts aggressively. The data proves they are a highly effective customer acquisition tool that does not create a "cheap" customer base.

Part 2: The Profit Protector. Recommendation: Double down on Mobile and improve the mid-length browsing experience. Since mobile drives most sales and customers browsing 5-16 minutes show slightly lower satisfaction, optimizing these areas protects and enhances revenue.

Part 3: The Loyalty Loop. Recommendation: Launch a systematic program to convert first-time buyers into fans. With an 86% repeat rate, the focus should be on nurturing new customers immediately after their first purchase, especially in high-loyalty categories like Beauty and Electronics.

In essence, this project moves seamlessly from data extraction (SQL), to insight generation (Analysis Report), to strategic action (Proposal). It tells a clear story: discounts, when analyzed with data, are not a threat to brand value but a powerful lever for profitable growth, especially when combined with a superior mobile experience and a smart customer retention system.
